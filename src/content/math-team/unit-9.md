# Unit 9: Competition Tips & Speed Tricks

The formulas live in Units 4–8, and proof techniques in Unit 10. This unit is about **how** to use them under time pressure.

## Personal Tips for Competition Day ⭐
<!-- contests: mathcounts oml amc8 amc10 amc12 aime -->

1. **Read the whole question, then the answer blank.** The blank tells you the form: units, $x=$, an ordered pair, or set notation.
2. **Do the easy problems first.** On a Sprint round or an OML event, bank the A-level points in under a minute each, and save the time for the hard ones.
3. **Write neatly on scratch paper.** Most errors are copying errors, not math errors.
4. **Check by plugging back in**, especially for radical, log, rational, and absolute-value equations (extraneous roots!).
5. **Simplify completely**, in the exact form requested. An unsimplified correct answer is usually marked wrong.
6. **Guess on multiple choice only when** there's no penalty (on the AMC 10/12, a blank is worth 1.5 points and a wrong answer 0; the AMC 8 and AIME have no penalty).
7. **Don't get stuck.** If you've spent two minutes with no progress, move on and come back.

## Problem-Solving Strategies ⭐
<!-- contests: all -->

- **Draw a picture**, even for algebra and counting problems.
- **Try small cases**, find the pattern, then prove or extend it.
- **Work backwards** from the answer form: "write as $\frac{\log a}{\log b}$" tells you the path.
- **Plug in numbers** to test identities, or to check an "in terms of $k$" answer.
- **Look for symmetry and invariants.** If swapping $x$ and $y$ doesn't change the problem, the answer often has $x=y$.
- **Complementary counting** whenever you see "at least one".
- **Name things.** Assign variables to unknown lengths and write equations.
- **Use extremes:** the smallest or largest case often reveals the structure.

## Speed and Accuracy Habits
<!-- contests: mathcounts oml amc8 amc10 amc12 -->

- Know the squares to $30^2$, cubes to $10^3$, powers of 2 to $2^{12}$, the primes under 100, and the factorials to $10!$.
- Know the decimal equivalents of $\frac18$ through $\frac78$, the sixths, the ninths, and the elevenths.
- Leave answers in radical/fraction form until the end. Never round in the middle of a problem.
- Estimate first, so a wildly wrong answer jumps out.

## Countdown / Speed-Round Tricks
<!-- contests: mathcounts -->

| Trick | Example |
|---|---|
| Squaring numbers ending in 5: $\overline{n5}^2 = n(n+1)$ followed by $25$ | $65^2=42\,25$ |
| Difference of squares | $47\times53=50^2-3^2=2491$ |
| Multiplying near 100 | $97\times96=(100-3)(100-4)=9312$ |
| Multiplying by 11: add neighboring digits | $11\times 243=2673$ |
| Multiplying by 25: ×100 ÷4 | $25\times 36=900$ |
| Squares near 50: $(50+k)^2=2500+100k+k^2$ | $53^2=2809$ |
| Sum of an arithmetic sequence = average × count | $3+7+\dots+43=23\times11=253$ |
| $\frac17=0.\overline{142857}$, and $\frac k7$ is a rotation of those digits | $\frac37=0.\overline{428571}$ |
| Percent swap: $a\%$ of $b$ = $b\%$ of $a$ | $8\%$ of $25$ = $25\%$ of $8$ = $2$ |

## AMC 8 / 10 / 12 Strategy ⭐
<!-- contests: amc8 amc10 amc12 -->

| | AMC 8 | AMC 10 | AMC 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | grade 8 and below | grade 10 and below | grade 12 and below |
| Format | 25 questions, 40 min | 25 questions, 75 min | 25 questions, 75 min |
| Scoring | 1 per correct, no penalty | 6 correct, 1.5 blank, 0 wrong (max 150) | same as AMC 10 |
| Leads to | — | AIME | AIME |

AMC 10 and 12 each run twice a year (the **A** and **B** dates, in November). You may take one A and one B and use your better score.

- **Pacing:** aim to finish problems 1–10 in about 20 minutes with no mistakes. That leaves real time for 11–25.
- **Accuracy beats speed.** A wrong answer is worth 1.5 points less than a blank. Only guess when you can eliminate at least two choices (on the AMC 10/12).
- **On the AMC 8 there is no penalty**, so never leave a blank.
- **Use the answer choices:** plug them in, check parity or units digits, estimate to rule out choices, and test small cases.
- **Skip and return.** Problems are roughly in order of difficulty, but not exactly. Read 20–25 before giving up on them; one may be easy for you.
- **Bubble carefully** and recheck the question number every five problems.

> [!tip] Score goals
> AIME qualification usually needs roughly **100–110 on the AMC 10** or **80–95 on the AMC 12** (cutoffs change every year, so check the AoPS wiki for the latest). On the AMC 10, 15–16 correct with the rest left blank is 105–109.5.

## AIME Strategy
<!-- contests: aime -->

- **Format:** 15 problems, 3 hours, every answer an integer from 000 to 999, no penalty for guessing, no calculators.
- **Your goal depends on your path:** your USAJMO/USAMO index is your AMC score + 10 × your AIME score, so each AIME problem is worth 10 AMC points.
- **12 minutes per problem on average**, but problems 1–5 should go faster. Solve them first, then pick the later ones whose topics you're strongest in.
- **The answer must be an integer 0–999.** If you get a fraction or a negative number, you've probably misread the question ("find $m+n$").
- **Write a guess for every problem** in the last minute. There's no penalty.
- Topics to know well: Vieta and polynomials, logs, complex numbers and roots of unity, counting with bijections and recursion, modular arithmetic, and area/length chasing in geometry.

## USAJMO / USAMO: Proof Contests
<!-- contests: usajmo usamo -->

- **Format:** two days, 3 problems per day, 4.5 hours per day. Each problem is graded **0–7**; total 42.
- **Who:** invited from the top AMC + AIME scorers. The **USAJMO** is for students who qualified through the AMC 10; the **USAMO** through the AMC 12.
- **Every answer is a proof.** A correct answer with no justification is worth almost nothing. A clear partial argument can still earn points.
- **The usual problem types:** one each of algebra (inequalities, functional equations, polynomials), combinatorics, geometry, and number theory, at increasing difficulty (problems 1 and 4 are the "easiest").
- **Spend the first 30 minutes understanding**: test small cases, draw accurate figures, guess the answer.
- **Write up as you go** so you don't lose a solved problem to the clock.

See **Unit 10** for proof techniques.

## Before Each Contest
<!-- contests: all -->

- Reread the ⭐ items for the topics being tested.
- Do one timed set from the practice bank under contest conditions.
- Redo every problem in the Question Bank's **Incorrect** tab.
- Sleep. A rested brain is worth more than an extra hour of cramming.
